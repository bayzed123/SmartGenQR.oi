What Is Keyword Density and Does It Matter?

Keyword density is how often a word or phrase appears in your content relative to the total word count. Modern SEO doesn't reward hitting a specific density target the way it did a decade ago — but wildly uneven density (either near-zero mentions of your target topic, or obvious over-repetition) is still a useful signal that something's off with your content.

How to Check Keyword Density

  1. Paste your article or content into the text box — a blog post, product page, or landing page draft.
  2. Review the frequency breakdown of individual words and phrases, ranked by how often they appear.
  3. Check your target keyword's density — if it barely appears, the page may be under-optimized; if it dominates the list unnaturally, it may read as keyword-stuffed.

Using Keyword Density the Right Way in 2026

1. There's no "ideal" density percentage anymore

Older SEO advice pushed a 1-3% target — modern search engines use semantic understanding, not a density formula. Write naturally, then use this tool as a sanity check, not a target to hit.

2. Watch for accidental over-repetition

It's easy to unconsciously repeat a phrase while drafting. If one term dominates the frequency list far above everything else, it's worth rereading for awkward, forced-sounding repetition.

3. Check related terms, not just your exact keyword

Content that naturally covers a topic tends to include related terms and synonyms, not just the exact target phrase repeated — look at your full frequency list for topical coverage, not one number.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What's a good keyword density percentage?

A: There's no universal correct number — modern search engines evaluate content semantically, not by density formula. Use this tool to spot obvious over- or under-use, not to hit a fixed target.

Q: Can keyword stuffing hurt my rankings?

A: Yes — unnaturally repetitive content is a known ranking penalty signal and reads poorly to actual readers, which hurts engagement metrics that also affect ranking.

Q: Does this analyze multi-word phrases too, or just single words?

A: Both — the breakdown includes individual words as well as common multi-word phrase repetition.
