What Do Terms & Conditions Actually Cover?

Terms & Conditions (also called Terms of Service) set the rules for using your site or app — acceptable use, account termination rights, limitation of liability, and governing law. It's the contract between you and your users, distinct from a Privacy Policy (data handling) or a Disclaimer (liability limits on specific content).

Making Your Terms Actually Enforceable

h3 style="color: #1a202c; margin-bottom: 10px;">1. Get explicit agreement, not just a link

A "by using this site you agree" footer link is weaker than an explicit checkbox at signup ("I agree to the Terms & Conditions") — the latter is much stronger evidence of consent if it's ever disputed.

h3 style="color: #1a202c; margin-bottom: 10px;">2. Pair it with a Privacy Policy

Terms & Conditions and a Privacy Policy cover different legal ground and most sites collecting any user data need both, not just one.

h3 style="color: #1a202c; margin-bottom: 10px;">3. Update it when your business changes

New features, payment terms, or a change in how disputes are handled should trigger a Terms update — notify existing users of material changes where required.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What's the difference between Terms & Conditions and a Privacy Policy?

A: Terms & Conditions govern how someone may use your site/service (rules, liability, termination). A Privacy Policy explains what personal data you collect and how it's used. Most sites need both, and they cover different topics.

Q: Is a generated Terms document legally binding?

A: A clear, published Terms document that users explicitly agree to (e.g. a checkbox at signup) is generally enforceable — but for a business handling significant liability or regulated activity, a lawyer's review is worth the cost.

Q: Can I customize the generated text?

A: Yes — the output is plain HTML you can edit to add clauses specific to your business before publishing.
