How to Preview Your Google Search Snippet

  1. Enter your Page Title — the exact text from your <title> tag, or click a card from Suggest with AI to fill it automatically.
  2. Enter your Page URL — shown as the green breadcrumb path under the title in results, and used as the domain when you check ranking.
  3. Enter your Meta Description — watch the preview to see exactly where it would truncate.
  4. Optional: Suggest with AI — type a one-line description of the page (e.g. "a free QR code generator") and get three ready-to-use title/description pairs, each with a different angle.

Writing Titles and Descriptions That Earn Clicks

1. Front-load the important keyword

Put your primary target keyword near the start of the title — both for clarity to the reader and because it's the part least likely to get cut off if the title runs long.

2. Google sometimes rewrites your title anyway

Google occasionally replaces your title tag with its own generated version if it thinks another phrasing better matches the search query — a clear, accurate title reduces how often this happens.

3. The description is your click-through pitch

It doesn't directly affect ranking, but a compelling, accurate description is what actually convinces someone to click your result over a competitor's.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why does my title get cut off even though it's under 60 characters?

A: Google truncates by pixel width, not character count — wide characters (like "W" or "M") take more space than narrow ones (like "i" or "l"), so the exact cutoff point varies by which letters are used.

Q: Does Google always use my written meta description?

A: Not always — Google sometimes generates its own snippet from page content if it judges that more relevant to the specific search query. Writing a strong, accurate description still improves the odds yours is used.

Q: Does this tool check my actual live page, or just what I type here?

A: The preview itself renders exactly what you type in — pair it with SmartGen's Meta Tag Generator to produce the actual HTML tags to publish. The ranking checker is different: it queries live Google search results for the keyword you enter.

Q: How does "Suggest with AI" work, and is it free?

A: It sends your one-line topic description to Google's Gemini model, which writes three title/description pairs already sized to Google's real display limits. It's free to use, with a fair-use hourly limit per visitor to keep it available for everyone.

Q: Why does the ranking checker only look at the top 10 results?

A: It's powered by the Google Custom Search API, which has a limited free daily quota shared across every visitor to this page — checking only the first page of results keeps that quota usable for everyone instead of one search burning through it. If your page isn't in the top 10, it's outside first-page results for that keyword.
