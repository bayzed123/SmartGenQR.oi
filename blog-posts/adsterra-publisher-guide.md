# Adsterra Review and Publisher Guide: The Complete A-to-Z Walkthrough for Bloggers

*Disclosure: this guide includes Adsterra referral links and official Adsterra promotional banners. Signing up through them costs you nothing extra and helps support this site — the comparison and advice below are the same either way.*

If you run a blog, a tool site, or any kind of content platform and you're looking for ways to earn from your traffic, two names come up constantly: Google AdSense and Adsterra. This guide walks through what Adsterra actually is, how it honestly compares to AdSense, and exactly how to get started — including the parts most promotional pages leave out.

## What Is Adsterra?

Adsterra is a global advertising network founded in 2013, connecting a large base of publishers (site owners who want to run ads) with advertisers who want to reach their traffic. It's grown into one of the larger self-serve ad networks in the industry, working with tens of thousands of direct publishers across a wide range of ad formats and payout models.

For publishers, it works the way most ad networks do: you sign up, add your site, get approved, paste a small piece of ad code onto your pages, and get paid based on how many people see or interact with the ads — depending on which pricing model your ad units use.

## Why Beginners Look at Adsterra Before (or Alongside) AdSense

This is the honest reason Adsterra shows up in nearly every "how to monetize your blog" conversation: **Google AdSense has a real approval bar.** It wants original content, reasonable traffic, and full policy compliance — and it rejects a lot of new sites, sometimes more than once, before accepting them.

Adsterra's approval process is dramatically more lenient. There's no minimum traffic requirement, and approval is often close to instant. That makes it a genuinely reasonable option if you're publishing consistently but haven't been accepted into AdSense yet, or if a meaningful share of your traffic comes from regions where AdSense's own fill rate and CPM tend to be weaker.

Worth being upfront about before you get excited: publisher discussions and independent comparisons consistently note that AdSense generally pays more per thousand views once you're eligible for it. Adsterra is best understood as a strong "start earning right now" option and a useful supplement — not necessarily a permanent replacement if AdSense is realistically within reach for your site.

Neither column is the universally "correct" choice — they solve different problems at different stages of a site's life, which is exactly why a lot of publishers end up running both.

## The Ad Formats Adsterra Offers — and Which Ones to Actually Use

Adsterra gives publishers more format variety than AdSense does, but not all of them are equally friendly to your readers or to running alongside AdSense cleanly:

- **Banner ads** — standard display units. Low-risk, familiar to readers, safe to run anywhere.
- **Native ads** — styled to blend with your site's content. Generally unobtrusive.
- **Social Bar** — an interactive notification-style format. Reasonably contained if placed carefully.
- **In-Page Push** — simulates a push notification within the page. More attention-grabbing than a banner.
- **Direct Links** — a link that sends a visitor to an advertiser's page when clicked, with no visible ad unit until it's clicked.
- **Popunder** — opens a new browser window or tab behind the current one. Historically one of Adsterra's highest-earning formats, and also the one that generates the most reader complaints when overused.
- **Interstitial** — a full-page ad shown between content views.

The honest recommendation: **if you're also running AdSense, or you simply care about reader trust, lead with Banner, Native, and Social Bar.** Google's own AdSense placement policy explicitly prohibits ads delivered as pop-ups, so running Popunders on pages that also carry AdSense is a real risk to your AdSense standing, not just a UX nitpick. If you want to test Popunder or Interstitial for the extra revenue, keep them isolated to pages or sections that don't run AdSense at all.

## Step-by-Step: Signing Up and Adding the Code

Signing up costs nothing — Adsterra doesn't charge publishers to join.

1. **Create your account.** Sign up through [this Adsterra referral link](https://beta.publishers.adsterra.com/referral/jcz6DrZ9pY) and select "Publisher" during registration.
2. **Add your website.** Enter your domain and basic details about your content and traffic.
3. **Wait for review.** Approval is frequently instant and rarely takes more than 24 hours for a normal, policy-compliant site — no waiting weeks like a first-time AdSense application often involves.
4. **Pick your ad format(s).** Start with Banner or Native for the safest option, based on the placement guidance above.
5. **Copy your ad code.** Adsterra generates a small JavaScript snippet for each ad unit you create in the dashboard.
6. **Paste the code into your site.** On WordPress, this usually means a header/footer script plugin, a widget area, or directly into your theme's template, depending on where you want the unit to appear.
7. **Confirm it's live.** Check the page — ideally in a private/incognito browser window, since ad networks don't always serve test ads to your own logged-in session — to confirm the unit is actually rendering.

That's the entire technical lift: no coding beyond pasting a snippet where you want the ad to appear.

## The Honest Pros and Cons

**What Adsterra genuinely does well:**
- Fast, low-barrier approval with no traffic minimum — a real, verified advantage for new sites
- Multiple payout methods with a low minimum threshold
- Weekly payouts once your account is established, versus AdSense's monthly cycle
- Strong fill rates for global and Tier-2/Tier-3 traffic that AdSense often monetizes weakly
- An established company — it's been operating since 2013, not a fly-by-night operation

**What to know before you commit:**
- CPM is generally lower than AdSense for comparable Tier-1 traffic — set expectations accordingly
- The higher-earning formats (Popunder, Interstitial) are intrusive enough to hurt reader experience if overused, and can conflict with AdSense's own placement rules
- Publisher reviews on independent platforms show a recurring pattern of complaints about withheld balances or account suspensions — this isn't unique to Adsterra among networks in this tier, but it's real enough that you should read the terms of service carefully, keep your own records, and avoid treating any single ad network as your only income stream
- Payout experience can vary by account manager, GEO, and niche — one publisher's report isn't a guarantee of what you'll see

**Who might want to skip it, at least for now:** if your site already has approved, healthy AdSense revenue from mostly Tier-1 traffic, the added complexity of a second network may not be worth the marginal revenue. Adsterra earns its place mainly for sites that can't get AdSense yet, or that have meaningful traffic AdSense doesn't monetize well.

## Running Adsterra and AdSense Together, Safely

Google explicitly permits non-Google ad networks on the same site or page as AdSense — this isn't a gray area, it's stated directly in Google's own publisher documentation. Two things actually matter for staying compliant:

1. **Ad density.** Your content needs to outweigh your ads on the page — don't stack multiple large ad units on top of each other.
2. **Placement rules.** No ads that encourage accidental clicks, no misleading labels, and critically, no ads delivered as pop-ups on any page carrying AdSense — exactly why the format guidance above steers you toward Banner, Native, and Social Bar on those pages.

Handled that way, running both is a common, unremarkable setup. Many publishers run AdSense on their main content and lean on Adsterra's other formats to monetize the traffic AdSense fills poorly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Adsterra free to join?**
Yes. There's no cost to sign up or to add ad code to your site as a publisher.

**Do I need a minimum amount of traffic?**
No. Adsterra has no stated traffic minimum, which is the main reason it's popular with new sites that don't yet qualify for AdSense.

**How fast is approval?**
Commonly within 24 hours, and often close to instant for a normal, policy-compliant site.

**Can I use Adsterra and AdSense on the same website?**
Yes — Google explicitly allows non-Google ad networks alongside AdSense, as long as you follow AdSense's ad density and placement rules, in particular avoiding pop-up-style formats on any page that also runs AdSense.

**Which ad format should I start with?**
Banner or Native, if you want the format least likely to annoy readers or conflict with AdSense. Popunders and Interstitials generally pay more per impression but are more intrusive.

**How and when do I get paid?**
Adsterra supports multiple payment methods with a minimum payout as low as $5 depending on the method, moving to weekly payouts once your account is established.

**Should I drop AdSense once I'm approved, and just use Adsterra?**
Generally no — AdSense typically pays more for the traffic it monetizes well. The more common, more profitable setup is running both, with AdSense on your main content and Adsterra's gentler formats filling in elsewhere.

Adsterra isn't a replacement for AdSense, and it isn't trying to be one — it's a fast, low-barrier way to start earning from a site AdSense hasn't accepted yet, or to pick up revenue from traffic AdSense doesn't monetize well. Used with the placement guidance above, it's a reasonable, honest addition to a blog's monetization stack.
