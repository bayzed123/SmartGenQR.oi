Percentage Math Worth Knowing

1. "Percent of" vs. "percent difference" aren't the same

Finding 20% of $100 ($20) is a different calculation from asking how much a price changed by percent — mixing these up is one of the most common percentage mistakes.

2. Stacked discounts don't add up the way you'd expect

Two separate 10% discounts don't equal a flat 20% off — they compound, resulting in a slightly smaller total discount (19%) than adding the percentages directly.

3. Percent change direction matters

A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease doesn't return you to the original number — the base value changes between the two calculations, so the two 50%'s apply to different amounts.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: How do I calculate a discount price manually?

A: Multiply the original price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). For example, $80 with a 25% discount: 80 × (1 − 0.25) = $60.

Q: What's the formula for percent difference?

A: (New Value − Old Value) ÷ Old Value × 100. A positive result is an increase; negative is a decrease.

Q: Can I calculate reverse percentages, like finding the original price before a discount?

A: Yes conceptually — if you know the discounted price and the discount percentage, divide the discounted price by (1 − discount ÷ 100) to recover the original price.
