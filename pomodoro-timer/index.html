What Is the Pomodoro Technique?

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method built around short, focused work sprints (traditionally 25 minutes) followed by a short break (traditionally 5 minutes) — the fixed structure reduces the mental friction of "when should I stop" and makes sustained focus easier to maintain.

Set your work and break durations and this timer counts down each session with a clear on-screen label, right in your browser tab.

How to Use the Pomodoro Timer

  1. Set your Work Duration (default 25 minutes) and Break Duration (default 5 minutes).
  2. Start the timer and focus on a single task until the work session ends.
  3. Take the break when it starts — step away from the screen rather than continuing to work through it.
  4. Repeat, and after roughly four cycles, take a longer break (15-30 minutes) to avoid burnout.

Getting the Most Out of Pomodoro Sessions

1. One task per session

Pick a single task before starting the timer, not a general block of "work" — a defined task makes the 25 minutes feel purposeful and easier to focus on.

2. Actually take the break

Skipping breaks to "keep momentum" defeats the technique's purpose — the short break is what prevents fatigue over a long work session.

3. Adjust the duration to your own attention span

25 minutes is a starting point, not a rule — some people focus better with 50-minute sessions and longer breaks. Experiment with the duration settings until it fits how you actually work.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why 25 minutes specifically?

A: It's the interval the technique's creator, Francesco Cirillo, originally settled on as long enough to make progress but short enough to sustain focus without fatigue — it's a convention, not a hard rule.

Q: What should I do during the break?

A: Step away from the screen — stretch, get water, look out a window. Continuing to scroll a phone during a "break" tends to defeat the mental reset the technique is meant to provide.

Q: Does the timer keep running if I switch browser tabs?

A: Yes — the countdown continues in the background as long as the tab stays open, even if it's not the active tab.
