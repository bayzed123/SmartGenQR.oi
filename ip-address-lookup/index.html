What Does an IP Address Lookup Show You?

Your public IP address is the address your router presents to the wider internet — different from the private address (like 192.168.x.x) your device uses on your home network. This tool shows your current public IP along with basic network details like your approximate location and ISP, straight from your browser.

The lookup queries a public IP geolocation API directly from your browser — SmartGen's own servers never see your address.

Common Reasons to Check Your IP

1. Verifying a VPN is actually working

Connect to your VPN, then check this page — your public IP and location should change to match the VPN server, not your real location.

2. Whitelisting an IP for server or firewall access

Server admins often need a visitor's exact public IP to add a firewall rule or grant access to a restricted service.

3. Troubleshooting network or geolocation issues

If a website is showing you the wrong country or region, checking your public IP's geolocation here helps confirm whether it's an ISP routing issue.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why is my location shown slightly off?

A: IP geolocation is approximate — it's based on your ISP's registered address ranges, which are often mapped to a regional hub rather than your exact physical location.

Q: Does this tool see or store my IP address?

A: The lookup is made directly from your browser to a third-party IP geolocation API — SmartGen's own servers are not involved in the request and don't log your IP.

Q: What's the difference between my public and private IP?

A: Your private IP (often 192.168.x.x or 10.x.x.x) only exists on your local network. Your public IP, shown here, is what the wider internet sees you as — usually assigned by your ISP or shared across your household's devices.
